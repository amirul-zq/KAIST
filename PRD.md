## 1. Project Overview

A browser-based, 3D reimagining of the traditional Korean board game **Yutnori (윷놀이)**, built with HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, and Three.js. The project is a class assignment focused on demonstrating game-logic architecture, state management, and real-time 3D rendering, while preserving the authentic rules and visual character of the traditional game. The game runs entirely client-side with no installation and no backend.

## 2. Target Users

- **Primary:** Two local players sharing one computer/screen, taking turns via mouse/keyboard (hot-seat multiplayer).
- **Secondary (bonus):** A single player against a built-in AI opponent.
- **Context of use:** Classroom downtime, breaks, casual play with friends — sessions should start instantly and finish within a few minutes.
- **Technical expectation:** Users have a modern desktop browser (Chrome/Edge/Firefox); no assumption of gaming hardware or high-end GPU.

## 3. Core Gameplay

0. Before the board loads, players complete a brief setup step: nicknames, language (EN/KO), and a piece face/character each (§19).
1. Players alternate turns throwing 4 Yut sticks.
2. The throw result determines how far a chosen piece moves along the board.
3. A player may choose which of their own pieces to move if more than one is in play.
4. Pieces can catch opposing pieces (sending them back to start) or stack with a player's own pieces (moving together).
5. Landing on a corner or the center node opens a diagonal shortcut toward home.
6. Bonus throws (from Yut, Mo, or a catch) must all be resolved — and every resulting move applied — before the turn passes to the other player.
7. The first player to move all 4 pieces around the board and off (home) wins.
8. A restart flow resets the board for a new match.

## 4. Complete Yutnori Rules

- 4 sticks (윷가락) are thrown together each turn; each stick lands flat-side-up or round-side-up.
- The combination of flat/round sides determines the throw result (see §5).
- Each player has 4 pieces (말) that start off-board and must travel a full lap around the board (using the outer path and/or diagonal shortcuts) back to the start/home node to be borne off.
- A player keeps throwing and moving as long as they keep earning bonus throws (Yut, Mo, or catching an opponent); the turn only passes once a throw yields no bonus.
- A piece that lands exactly on a shortcut-enabled node (a corner or the center) automatically continues home via the diagonal shortcut on subsequent moves, rather than the long way around the outer ring.
- Landing on a space occupied by an opponent's single piece or stack **catches** it — the caught piece(s) return to start, and the catching player earns a bonus throw.
- Landing on a space occupied by the player's own piece **stacks** them; stacked pieces move and are caught together as one unit from then on.
- A player who cannot legally move (e.g., no pieces on board and no new throw pending) simply passes after their throw resolves.

## 5. Do, Gae, Geol, Yut, Mo, and Back Do

| Throw | Korean | Stick pattern (of 4) | Spaces moved | Grants bonus throw |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do | 도 | 1 flat, 3 round | 1 | No |
| Gae | 개 | 2 flat, 2 round | 2 | No |
| Geol | 걸 | 3 flat, 1 round | 3 | No |
| Yut | 윷 | 4 flat | 4 | **Yes** |
| Mo | 모 | 4 round | 5 | **Yes** |
| Back-do | 백도 | 1 flat, 3 round — same physical count as Do, but the flat-side-up stick is the specially marked one | 1 (**backward**) | No |

- **Do vs. Back-do disambiguation:** one of the 4 sticks is a marked stick (traditionally a notch/ink mark). Both Do and Back-do occur on a "1 flat / 3 round" throw; the result is **Do** if the flat stick is one of the 3 unmarked sticks, and **Back-do** if the flat stick is specifically the marked one. The engine must track stick identity (not just a flat/round count) to resolve this correctly.
- Back-do moves the chosen piece one space *backward* along the path it came from. If a piece is at the start with nothing to move backward, the throw is forfeited (no movement, no penalty).
- Throw outcomes are generated by independently randomizing each stick's face, then mapping the combination to a result per the table above.

## 6. Movement Values

- Movement value = spaces advanced along the current path (outer ring or active diagonal shortcut) in the forward direction, except Back-do which is 1 space in the reverse direction.
- Stacked pieces move together and consume a single throw's movement value as one unit.
- Movement is always computed against the board graph (§10–12), which resolves whether the move continues on the outer ring or transitions onto a shortcut.

## 7. Bonus Throws

- Triggered by: rolling **Yut**, rolling **Mo**, or **catching** an opponent's piece.
- Each bonus throw is queued; the player throws again immediately.
- Multiple bonus throws can stack in the same turn (e.g., Yut followed by another Yut, or Yut followed by a catch).
- The turn does not pass to the other player until a throw is made that produces **no** bonus (i.e., Do, Gae, Geol, or Back-do with no catch).

## 8. Complete All Bonus Throws Before Movement

To match traditional play and avoid ambiguous partial-turns:
- All queued bonus throws are resolved (thrown) **first**, producing a full list of pending move values for the current player.
- Only after the bonus-throw queue is empty does the player begin applying moves — choosing, for each pending value (in the order earned), which piece to move.
- This means a player might throw Yut, then Mo (a second bonus), then Gae (ends the throwing phase) — resulting in 3 pending moves (4, 5, 2) to apply in sequence before the turn passes, and any catches made *while applying these moves* add further bonus throws that get resolved (thrown) before movement resumes, following the same rule recursively.
- Rationale: keeps rendering and turn-state simple — a clean "throwing phase" fully separated from a "movement phase" per turn segment.

## 9. Board Layout

The board is modeled as a graph of 29 distinct nodes: a 20-node outer ring plus 5 shared diagonal/center nodes, matching traditional Yutnori board topology.

```
        O10 (corner)
   O9 ..      .. O11
  O8            O12
  O7   D0  C  D10 O13
  O6   |  / \  |  O14
  O5---D5-C--D15--O15  (C = shared CENTER node)
  O4            O16
  O3            O17
  O2 ..      .. O18
        O1  O0=START/HOME  O19
```

*(Simplified schematic — see §10–12 for precise node definitions.)*

## 10. Outer Path

- 20 nodes: `O0` … `O19`, arranged clockwise around the square board.
- `O0` is both the **Start** node (where all pieces begin, off-board conceptually "at" O0) and the **Home** entry node (a piece that would move past O0 is instead borne off / completed).
- Corners are at `O0`, `O5`, `O10`, `O15` (each edge of the board has 5 outer nodes: a corner plus 4 in-between nodes).
- Standard movement along the outer ring: `O0 → O1 → O2 → … → O19 → (home)`.

## 11. Diagonal Shortcuts

- Two diagonals cross at a single shared **Center** node (`C`), connecting opposite corners:
  - Diagonal A: `O5 ↔ D5 ↔ C ↔ D10 ↔ O10`
  - Diagonal B: `O0 ↔ D0 ↔ C ↔ D15 ↔ O15` *(entry from the O0/start side is only reachable when a piece re-enters via C from elsewhere; O0 itself is the home node)*
- A piece landing exactly on `O5`, `O10`, `O15`, or `C` automatically takes the shortcut toward home on its next move(s), since the diagonal route is shorter than continuing around the outer ring.
- From `C`, the shortest path home is `C → D15 → O15 → O16 → O17 → O18 → O19 → home`... engine always computes the shortest legal remaining path to home from the piece's current node given the move's remaining spaces.

## 12. Center Path

- The **Center** node (`C`) is the hub where both diagonals cross.
- A piece may pass through `C` as part of a longer move (e.g., landing beyond `D5` with enough remaining spaces continues through `C` toward `D10`/`O10` or `D15`/`O15`, whichever is the correct forward direction toward home).
- Landing exactly on `C` also grants shortcut eligibility, per §11.

## 13. Piece Selection

- After each pending move value is ready to apply, if the current player has more than one eligible piece (on board or at start), the UI presents a selectable list/highlight of that player's pieces.
- The player clicks/selects a piece; the engine validates the move (including catch/stack resolution) and animates it.
- If only one piece is eligible, selection is automatic (skip the prompt) to keep pace during simple turns.

## 14. Catching

- If a move ends on a node occupied by opponent piece(s), those opponent piece(s) are sent back to Start (`O0`, off-board state) — regardless of whether the opponent's pieces were stacked.
- The catching player is granted one bonus throw (queued per §7–8).
- A player cannot catch their own pieces (that triggers stacking instead, §15).

## 15. Stacking

- If a move ends on a node occupied by the mover's own piece(s), the pieces merge into a single stack.
- A stack moves as one unit on all subsequent turns and is caught or scored as a whole.
- (Stretch/bonus, not required for MVP: allowing a stack to split back into individual pieces on a future move.)

## 16. Completion and Winning

- A piece completes its lap when a move would take it to or past the Home node (`O0`) after having traveled the board; it is then marked **Home** and removed from active play.
- The first player to get all 4 pieces Home wins immediately; the game stops accepting further input except "Restart."
- A win banner/UI state displays the winning player and offers Restart.

## 17. Restart

- A visible "Restart" control is available at all times (not just after a win), with a confirmation step if a game is in progress (to avoid accidental resets).
- Restart resets: piece positions, turn order (back to Player 1), pending-throw queue, and any UI/animation state, without requiring a page reload.

## 18. Turn-State Flow

```
[GAME START] → [SETUP: nickname / language / piece-face selection, §19] → [TURN START: Player 1]
      │
      ▼
[TURN START: current player]  ── HUD turn indicator updates immediately (§19)
      │
      ▼
 [THROW STICKS] ──► resolve result (Do/Gae/Geol/Yut/Mo/Back-do)
      │
      ▼
 Bonus throw earned (Yut/Mo)? ──yes──► queue another throw ──► [THROW STICKS]
      │ no
      ▼
 [ALL QUEUED THROWS RESOLVED] (§8) — no movement has happened yet at this point
      │
      ▼
 [APPLY PENDING MOVES] (in order earned)
      │  for each pending move:
      │    - select piece (§13)
      │    - resolve path via board graph (§9-12)
      │    - resolve catch (§14) → catch found?
      │        └─ yes → immediately throw the bonus stick-throw right now (interrupting
      │                 the remaining pending-move queue), append its result to
      │                 pending moves, THEN resume applying pending moves (§8, recursive)
      │    - resolve stack (§15)
      │    - animate movement
      │
      ▼
 All 4 pieces Home? ──yes──► [GAME OVER: show winner + victory effect, offer Restart (§17, §26)]
      │ no
      ▼
 [SWITCH TO OTHER PLAYER] → HUD turn indicator flips → [TURN START]
```

Note on §8 vs. mid-turn catches: "complete all bonus throws before movement" is guaranteed for Yut/Mo — those are always known and fully resolved before the first piece of the turn moves. A catch can only be discovered *after* a move has already happened, so its bonus throw is necessarily resolved immediately at that point (not deferred) before any further pending moves in the same turn are applied — this keeps the same "throw-then-move" discipline recursively, without ever leaving a bonus throw un-thrown while movement continues.

## 19. UI Layout

- **Pre-game setup screen** (shown once before the board loads, and reachable again from Restart):
  - Nickname text input per player (falls back to "Blue"/"Red" if left blank).
  - Language toggle: English / Korean (한국어), affecting all HUD labels and rule-result names (e.g., show "Yut (윷)" vs "Yut" depending on setting).
  - Piece face/character selection: each player picks one of a small preset gallery of face icons/patterns to decorate their 4 pieces (see §20 for how this is applied to the 3D piece geometry); a plain color-only option remains available as the default/fallback.
  - Optional: enable AI opponent toggle (bonus, §29), in place of Player 2.
- **Main viewport:** full-window 3D canvas (board, pieces, sticks) — the primary visual focus, laid out so the entire 29-node board is always visible and unobstructed by HUD elements (clear board legibility: node markers, corners, and the center are visually distinct from ordinary path spaces at a glance).
- **HUD overlay (non-blocking, corners of screen):**
  - Top: **turn indicator** — large, high-contrast banner showing the current player's nickname, color swatch, and chosen face icon (e.g., "🔵 Blue's Turn"), plus the pending-throws counter; this element changes immediately and unmistakably on every turn switch (§18) so players never have to guess whose turn it is.
  - Bottom-center: "Throw Sticks" button — visually disabled/greyed out (with a tooltip like "Move your pieces first") during the move-application phase, and re-enabled only when a throw is actually legal, so the control never invites an out-of-turn click.
  - Bottom-corner: mute/volume toggle.
  - Persistent small "Restart" control (top-corner).
- **Modal/banner (only on game end):** winner announcement (nickname + face icon) + victory effect (§26) + "Play Again" button.
- **Piece selection (intuitive controls):** handled via direct 3D-object click/hover highlighting on the board itself, not a separate list (keeps focus on the 3D scene); hovering a legal piece shows a glow/outline and a hand cursor, illegal/opponent pieces show no hover affordance, and the selected piece pulses briefly before animating — so at every step it's visually obvious what can be clicked.

## 20. 3D Rendering Plan

- **Engine:** Three.js, using `PerspectiveCamera` with a tilted top-down angle (~50–60° from vertical) to mimic looking down at a physical board on a table.
- **Board:** a flat tilted plane/box mesh with a wood-toned material; node positions mapped to 3D world coordinates (see §23) with subtle raised markers for corners/center.
- **Pieces:** primitive geometry (e.g., cones or short cylinders topped with a sphere) colored blue/red per player, with a distinguishing shape/pattern in addition to color (accessibility, §24). The top-facing surface of each piece renders the player's chosen **custom face** (a flat decal/texture from the setup-screen gallery, §19) via a simple canvas-generated texture applied to the mesh — no external model import required.
- **Sticks:** 4 elongated capsule/cylinder primitives with a flat-vs-round visual distinction (e.g., one face painted, or a flattened cross-section on one side); one of the 4 sticks additionally renders the traditional **marked-stick** indicator (a small painted dot/notch) so Back-do vs. Do is visually distinguishable to players watching the throw, not just internally tracked (§5). Sticks animate with a tumbling/toss motion (procedural or scripted keyframes) when thrown, settling into a flat/round-up resting rotation matching the resolved result.
- **Lighting:** one soft directional light (sun-like, casting soft shadows) + ambient fill light to avoid harsh contrast; `PCFSoftShadowMap` for shadow quality.
- **Background & Korean atmosphere:** dark green (felt-table-like) flat background or gradient; the board's outer border renders a simple traditional Korean trim pattern (e.g., a repeating dancheong-style geometric band, built from primitive shapes/textures, not photographic assets); on-board corner/center markers use a hanji-paper or wood-grain texture rather than plain flat color; HUD headings use a Korean-friendly font stack and show bilingual labels when the Korean language option is selected (§19).
- **Camera:** mostly fixed/static per the "local players share one screen" design, with light easing/zoom on key moments (throw, catch, win); no free-fly camera required for MVP.
- **Performance target:** all primitive geometry (no heavy imported models required for MVP) to keep it lightweight and framework-appropriate for a class assignment.

## 21. File Structure

Flat, root-level structure — no bundler, no nested `js/` tree. Everything lives directly in the repo root alongside the untouched `self-introduction/` project, and is loaded via plain ES module `<script type="module">` tags (no build step).

```
KAIST/

├── README.md
├── EVALUATION.md              # assignment self-grading checklist (mirrors §28)
├── self-introduction/         (existing, unrelated prior assignment — untouched)
├── index.html                 # page shell; import map for Three.js (CDN) + entry script
├── style.css                  # layout, dark-green background, HUD/setup-screen positioning
├── main.js                    # entry point: Three.js scene/camera/lighting/render loop;
│                               # wires gameLogic.js + boardData.js + ui.js together
├── gameLogic.js                # pure rules + state — throws, movement, catch/stack, turn
│                               # flow (§4-8, §13-18, §22); no DOM, no Three.js
├── boardData.js                # board graph: node ids, topology, 3D coordinates only
│                               # (§9-12, §23); no rules, imported (read-only) by gameLogic.js
├── ui.js                       # DOM/HUD only — setup screen, turn indicator, buttons,
│                               # winner banner (§19); reads state, never mutates it,
│                               # never touches Three.js
├── i18n.js                    # English/Korean string lookup used by ui.js (§19)
├── assets/
│   ├── textures/               # wood/hanji/board-trim textures (§20)
│   ├── sounds/                 # throw, catch, stack, win, ambient audio (§26)

```

Notes on responsibilities that will grow inside existing files rather than spawning new ones, to keep the structure flat:
- **Stick-throw randomization** (§5) and **AI move-choice logic** (bonus, §29) both belong in `gameLogic.js`, kept as clearly separated functions/sections rather than new top-level files.
- **Sound playback** (§26) and **piece/stick move animation** (§20) are driven from `main.js`'s render loop and triggered by `ui.js` event handlers, reading cues from `gameLogic.js` state — not split into separate `render/` or `audio/` files.
- If any of these areas grow large enough to hurt readability, prefer splitting by concern (e.g., a `render.js` for the render loop) while keeping the same flat, no-build-step convention — not reintroducing nested folders.

## 22. Game-State Data Structure

```js
GameState = {
  settings: {
    language: 'en' | 'ko',
    aiEnabled: boolean
  },
  players: [
    {
      id: 'blue' | 'red',
      nickname: string,        // defaults to "Blue"/"Red" if left blank at setup
      faceId: string,          // selected piece-face/character id from the setup gallery (§19-20)
      isAI: boolean,
      pieces: [
        { id: string, position: NodeId | 'START' | 'HOME', stackId: string | null }
        // 4 entries per player
      ]
    },
    // ...second player
  ],
  currentPlayerIndex: 0 | 1,
  turnPhase: 'SETUP' | 'THROWING' | 'APPLYING_MOVES' | 'GAME_OVER',
  throwQueue: [ ThrowResult ],     // bonus throws not yet thrown
  pendingMoves: [ ThrowResult ],   // thrown results awaiting a piece choice/application
  stacks: {
    [stackId]: { owner: 'blue' | 'red', pieceIds: [string], position: NodeId | 'START' | 'HOME' }
  },
  winner: 'blue' | 'red' | null,
  log: [ TurnEvent ]               // human-readable event history, for UI/debug
}

ThrowResult = {
  type: 'DO' | 'GAE' | 'GEOL' | 'YUT' | 'MO' | 'BACKDO',
  value: number,        // 1-5, or -1 for BACKDO
  isBonus: boolean
}
```

## 23. Board Coordinate Structure

```js
BoardNode = {
  id: string,                 // e.g. 'O0'..'O19', 'D0','D5','D10','D15', 'C'
  kind: 'start' | 'outer' | 'diagonal' | 'center',
  isCorner: boolean,
  worldPosition: { x: number, y: number, z: number },  // for Three.js placement
  next: string[]              // ids of legal forward node(s); >1 entry only at shortcut branch points
}
```

- The full board graph (29 nodes) is defined once as static data in `boardData.js`; `worldPosition` values are precomputed so the outer ring forms a square and the diagonal nodes align visually through the board's center, matching the tilted-board rendering plan (§20).
- Path resolution (given a start node + move value, forward or backward) is a graph traversal over `next` (or a precomputed `previous` map for Back-do), always preferring the shortcut branch when applicable per §11.

## 24. Accessibility

- **Keyboard support:** Throw action and piece selection (cycle + confirm) operable via keyboard (e.g., Space/Enter to throw, Tab/arrow keys to cycle selectable pieces, Enter to confirm), not mouse-only.
- **Color + shape redundancy:** blue/red pieces additionally differ in shape or icon/marker so colorblind users aren't relying on color alone.
- **Screen reader support:** an ARIA live region announces turn changes, throw results, catches, and the winner, mirroring the visual HUD state.
- **Contrast:** HUD text meets WCAG AA contrast against the dark green background.
- **Reduced motion:** a toggle (or `prefers-reduced-motion` detection) that shortens/skips stick-tumble and piece-slide animations for users sensitive to motion.
- **Audio control:** mute toggle and independent-of-browser volume control, since sound is a core feature (§26) but must not be mandatory for comprehension (visual/text feedback always accompanies audio cues).

## 25. Responsive Behavior

- Canvas and `PerspectiveCamera` aspect ratio update on window resize (`ResizeObserver`/`resize` listener), re-rendering at the new size without distortion.
- HUD elements use CSS flexbox/grid with relative units so they reflow across window sizes.
- Minimum supported viewport: ~1024×768 (this is an inherently shared-screen, sit-together local multiplayer game, not primarily a mobile experience); below a defined breakpoint, show a friendly "please use a larger screen" notice rather than a broken layout.
- Board tilt/camera distance adjusts slightly at narrower widths so the full board remains in frame.

## 26. Sound and Animation

- **Sound effects:** stick throw/clatter, piece slide/move, catch ("send back"), stack, win fanfare; all short, non-looping one-shots except optional low-volume ambient background loop (traditional Korean instrumentation, e.g., a light gugak-style loop, to reinforce the Korean atmosphere of §20).
- **Animation:**
  - Stick toss: tumbling rotation animation settling into the resolved flat/round pattern (~0.8–1.2s).
  - Piece movement: eased slide/hop along the board-graph path node-by-node (not a straight-line teleport across the board).
  - Catch: caught piece(s) animate a "knocked back" motion returning to Start.
  - **Victory effect (distinct, required beat):** on game-over, a dedicated sequence plays — brief celebratory camera move/zoom toward the winner's pieces, a lighting flourish (brighter/warmer light or particle-free color pulse), the win-fanfare sound, and the winner-announcement banner (§19) — clearly distinguishable from ordinary move/catch animations so a win is unmistakable even without reading the banner text.
- All animations are interruptible/skippable to keep pace during longer games (e.g., click-to-skip or auto-fast-forward if a player wants to speed through this animation), except the victory effect, which always plays in full.

## 27. Testing Strategy

- **Unit tests (rules engine, pure logic — no rendering):**
  - Throw-result mapping (all stick combinations → correct Do/Gae/Geol/Yut/Mo/Back-do + bonus flag).
  - Board graph traversal: forward movement across the outer ring, shortcut branching at each corner/center, backward movement for Back-do, and overflow/home-completion boundary cases.
  - Catch resolution (opponent piece(s), including stacks).
  - Stack resolution (own piece(s)).
  - Bonus-throw queuing and turn-switch conditions (§7–8, 18), including chained bonus scenarios.
  - Win-condition detection.
- **Manual/exploratory QA:**
  - Full playthrough to a win for both players.
  - Rendering checks: shadows, lighting, board tilt, piece/stick visual distinction across at least 2 browsers.
  - Animation and sound playback checks, including mute and reduced-motion toggles.
  - Responsive layout check at a few window sizes down to the minimum breakpoint.
- **Regression safety:** rules engine kept decoupled from Three.js so logic tests run headlessly (no browser/WebGL needed) in a fast test runner.
